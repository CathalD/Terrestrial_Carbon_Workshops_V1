## ⚠ The order matters more than anything else on this page

> **All vegetation work finishes before any coring.**

Coring is **destructive**. A corer hole in a quadrat you have not yet clipped is a lost plot, and
the trampling around it has already changed what you were about to measure.

A second rule, for **permanent plots** you intend to re-measure:

> **Offset the core from the quadrat, and record the offset.**

Your next visit has to avoid this visit's holes — and it has to sample soil that was not trampled
while someone knelt to clip.

**The working order at each plot:**

```
 1. Navigate, mark the plot, record GPS                ──┐
 2. Lay out the plot boundaries                          │
 3. Photo series                                         │  non-destructive
 4. Record management, grazing, fire, cover              │
 5. Trees, if canopy cover warrants   (3B → Forests)     │
 6. Shrubs — medium plot               (3B)              │
 7. Clip and weigh — small plot        (3B)            ──┘
 8. Soil core, offset from the quadrat                  ─┐  destructive
 9. Section by depth increment                           │
10. Bag for soil AND for roots                         ──┘
```

## Setting up a plot

### Navigate and mark

| | |
|---|---|
| **Plot sizes** | Medium **16–100 m²** (plants 0.5–2 m) · Small **0.25 m²** (below 0.5 m) · Large **400 m²** if trees are in scope |
| **Coordinates** | Record at the **plot centre**, with your **GNSS accuracy** |
| **Permanent plots** | Grassland has nothing to tag. Use a driven rod with a recorded depth, a buried magnet findable with a locator, **and** a bearing and distance from something durable. Photograph the setting |

### What goes on the plot log before you touch anything

Five fields that are peculiar to grassland and easy to skip:

| Field | How to record it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Grassland type** | Prairie / parkland / savannah / bunchgrass / other | Your stratification, and it drives whether 3B's tree section applies |
| **Management** | Grazed / ungrazed / hayed / cultivated-and-reseeded / never cultivated | **The largest single carbon difference you will find** |
| **Grazing regime** | None / season-long / rotational / heavy, plus stocking if known | Changes root allocation and surface compaction |
| **Years since fire** | Years, or "unknown", or "no recorded fire" | A stratum in savannah and parkland, not context |
| **Native or seeded** | Native sward / tame or introduced | Rooting depth differs, so the depth distribution of carbon differs |

> 🟠 **Most of this comes from the landholder, not from the site.** Have the conversation before
> the field day, not during it.

### The photo series

From the plot centre, following the same convention as the rest of the series:

| | Photos | Aim |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 1 | **Straight down** — the sward |
| **2** | 1 | **Straight up** — canopy, or sky |
| **3–14** | 12 | For **each cardinal direction**: one parallel with the ground, one **45° up**, one **45° down** |

**Plus two that are specific to this workshop:** the **quadrat before clipping** and the
**quadrat after**, from the same position. Those two frames are the only record of what the
standing crop looked like, and they take fifteen seconds.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Record the corer's internal diameter and write it on the corer.** It sets the sample volume
> and therefore every bulk density and every carbon stock. Out by 10% on the diameter puts every
> stock out by **21%**. Measure it with calipers once; put it on the sheet for every core.

## Timing

Two different constraints, and they do not always point to the same week.

| | When | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Vegetation** | **Peak growing season** | The only phenological point that is repeatable between sites and years. The [Vegetation guide](../../_Shared/Vegetation-FINAL-Eng-2026.pdf) specifies it |
| **Soil** | Fairly insensitive | Avoid saturated or frozen ground; both wreck bulk density |
| **Roots** | Follows the vegetation | Root biomass also varies seasonally, less violently. **Record the date** |

> [!WARNING]
> **If you must split the work across visits, split it at the soil.** Clip the vegetation and take
> the roots on the same trip — they are the two seasonal pools and they should share a date. Soil
> carbon will wait.

## Safety

Less hazardous than a peatland, but not nothing:

- **Heat and sun exposure.** Open grassland has no shade. This is the main one.
- **Livestock.** Cattle are curious and bulls are not safe. Know what is in the field before you
  enter it, and have the landholder's phone number.
- **Ticks.** Endemic in much of the prairie and interior BC. Check daily.
- **Rattlesnakes** in the south Okanagan, Thompson and parts of the dry mixedgrass. Watch where
  you put your hands when clearing a quadrat.
- **Fire risk** during the season you are most likely to be sampling. Vehicle exhaust in tall dry
  grass starts fires.
- **Gates and biosecurity.** Leave gates as you found them; clean boots between properties.
